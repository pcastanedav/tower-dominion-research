SECTION 5 — THE WAVE 63 SURVIVAL THRESHOLD
Status: draft for editorial review.
Source: SIMULATION-1000-RUNS.json, 1,000 runs per scenario.
NOTE: Wave 63 is a difficulty checkpoint; the model does not estimate survival.

5.1. Why Wave 63 matters

Wave 63 is the principal evaluation point because enemy wave size increases substantially around Waves 63–64.

A Mission Control strategy is most useful when its additional defensive options become available before that increase. Wave 40 introduces 3A Blueprint drafts, leaving the period from Waves 40 to 63 for the chain to develop.

A high Mission Control count reached much later demonstrates long-term acquisition potential, but does not necessarily help the player overcome the earlier transition.

All five scenarios show substantial growth between Waves 40 and 63. Lions maintains the highest average throughout. Eleonor remains second at Wave 63, while Magel moves ahead of Tander between Waves 50 and 60.

Averages alone do not show whether most runs reach a similar count or whether high-count runs pull the average upward. For this reason, the analysis also compares acquisition thresholds.

5.3. Probability of reaching Mission Control thresholds

The thresholds of 5, 10, and 20 Mission Controls are analytical reference points, not verified survival requirements.

Each probability is the share of the 1,000 simulated runs that acquired at least the indicated count by the end of Wave 63.

WAVE 63 THRESHOLD PROBABILITIES — NATURAL ACQUISITION
Scenario        >=5 MC   >=10 MC   >=20 MC
Baseline         67.0%     33.0%      6.1%
Lions            86.4%     69.0%     36.5%
Eleonor          84.9%     58.1%     16.7%
Tander           82.9%     51.2%     13.7%
Magel            76.5%     48.1%     18.9%

Lions leads at every threshold, especially at 20 Mission Controls, reached in 36.5% of runs.

Eleonor reaches at least ten in 58.1% of runs, followed by Tander at 51.2%.

Magel has a higher average than Tander but a lower chance of reaching five or ten. Conversely, Magel reaches twenty in 18.9% of runs, compared with 13.7% for Tander and 16.7% for Eleonor.

This indicates that Magel's higher average is associated with more high-count outcomes, not a uniformly higher likelihood of reaching every threshold.

5.4. Scenario comparison at Wave 63

Two different qualities matter: how frequently a strategy establishes a moderate chain, and how often it produces a particularly large chain.

Lions leads on both measures. Eleonor and Tander perform strongly at moderate thresholds. Magel is less consistent at reaching five or ten, but more frequently produces runs with twenty or more Mission Controls than either Eleonor or Tander.

Magel's additional Doctrine opportunities are an indirect acquisition advantage. Their value depends on which Doctrines are offered and when Amplifiers become available.

The baseline trails the scenarios, particularly at ten and twenty Mission Controls.

5.5. The effect of beginning with the Tier I Amplifier Doctrine

The alternative scenario starts with the Tier I Amplifier Doctrine already owned at Wave 1. That Doctrine is removed from later reward pools, while the Tier II Doctrine remains naturally obtainable. The maximum remains two Amplifiers.

WAVE 63: PROBABILITY OF >=10 MC
Scenario         Natural   Tier I from W1   Difference
Baseline           33.0%          36.1%        +3.1 pp
Lions              69.0%          73.7%        +4.7 pp
Eleonor            58.1%          65.0%        +6.9 pp
Tander             51.2%          56.7%        +5.5 pp
Magel              48.1%          50.4%        +2.3 pp

Every scenario improves in this sample. Eleonor has the largest gain in this measure, and Magel the smallest.

The modest change for Magel is consistent with his extra Doctrine drafts already providing additional opportunities to obtain Amplifier-granting Doctrines naturally. This is a plausible interpretation of the model results, not a separately isolated causal test.

5.6. Interpreting the results in relation to survival

These acquisition probabilities must not be presented as survival probabilities.

The model does not calculate enemy damage, defensive effectiveness, resource availability, or whether every acquired building can be constructed and upgraded in time. Nor does it establish a minimum MC count needed to survive Waves 63–64.

A run with fewer Mission Controls may still have an effective defense. A run with many may lack the necessary resources or supporting buildings.

The simulation instead estimates how frequently each scenario reaches different acquisition levels before the difficulty transition under its stated assumptions.

SECTION 5 SUMMARY

Wave 63 is the practical pre-escalation checkpoint. Lions reaches at least ten MCs in 69.0% of natural-acquisition runs and at least twenty in 36.5%.

Eleonor and Tander have strong moderate-threshold performance. Magel has more high-count outcomes, even though it reaches five or ten less often than those scenarios.

Guaranteeing the Tier I Amplifier Doctrine improves the ten-MC threshold for every commander, with the smallest gain for Magel.

Threshold probabilities reveal differences that averages alone conceal. They remain measures of acquisition, not survival.
